Print-film emulation LUTs — provenance
======================================

Files
-----
  kodak_2383_constlmap.cube   Kodak Vision Color Print Film 2383, the cinema print stock
  kodak_2393_constlmap.cube   Kodak Vision Premier 2393
  fuji_3510_constlmap.cube    Fujicolor Positive 3510
  fuji_3513_constlmap.cube    Fujicolor Eterna-CP 3513DI

Where they came from
--------------------
GitHub, downloaded 14 Sep 2026 — the same repository, and the same licence, as luts/looks/:

  https://github.com/YahiaAngelo/Film-Luts/tree/main/luts/print

Ultimately generated by G'MIC's film-emulation dataset (gmic.eu). luts/looks/SOURCE.txt once
recorded "a print-film emulation (Kodak 2383-class)" as unobtainable without a gated download. It
was sitting one folder over in the repository the looks came from; the search had looked for
Portra and nothing else.

Upstream ships each stock three ways — constlclip, constlmap and cuspclip, names for how colour
outside the print's gamut is brought inside it. Only constlmap is kept here. The other two were
not compared on this footage; that is a gap, not a finding.

What they expect, measured rather than assumed
----------------------------------------------
DISPLAY-REFERRED Rec.709 in, like the negative looks — not Cineon log, which is what a print
emulation in a colourist's tool usually expects. On IMG_0607 at 4s: fed Apple Log directly, or
Rec.709 re-encoded as Cineon printing density, the 2383 cube produced a flat, milky picture; fed
the conversion's Rec.709 it produced the print's contrast. So they sit in the chain after the look
and before the tone curve, as a second cube, and look.json's print.strength blends them back
toward their input because at full strength over a tuned tone curve the contrast doubles up.

All 13x13x13, like the looks, and read with tetrahedral interpolation for the same reason.
